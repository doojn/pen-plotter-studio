The Generative Recipe
1. Deterministic Seeds: Generative art uses pseudo-random numbers seeded by a specific number (like 4242). Every time you use that seed, the math reproduces the exact same strokes.
2. Vector Polylines: Instead of raster pixels, pen plotters only understand continuous coordinate points: [{x: 10, y: 15}, {x: 12, y: 18}, ...].
3. Physical Constraints: Unlike screens, physical pens have friction, paper absorbs ink, and pen lifts take mechanical time. Algorithms with fewer lifts plot faster and cleaner!

🌟 What is Algorithmic Generative Art?

Generative art is art made by a collaboration between a human artist and an autonomous system (an algorithm). Instead of manually drawing individual lines, you design the rules, mathematical formulas, and random variations. The computer computes thousands of coordinate points, and a mechanical pen plotter physically draws the artwork with real ink on paper!

🖋️ The 4 Steps: From Screen to Physical Paper

1
Choose & Tweak Your Algorithm: Pick an algorithm on the left sidebar (Flow Fields, Mountain Ridges, Harmonographs, etc.). Tweak the sliders, test starter recipes, and hit 🎲 Roll Seed until you love the composition.
2
Check Plotter Safety Margins: Keep the Safe Margin at 12mm or higher. This ensures the pen head never bumps into the metal binder clips holding your paper to the board!
3
Simulate the Draw: Click ▶️ Simulate Draw to watch the virtual pen move across your page. Look out for pen lifts and total distance.
4
Download SVG & Plot: Click 📥 Download SVG. Open the SVG in Inkscape, connect your AxiDraw or plotter via USB, select Extensions > AxiDraw > Plot, and watch your code come to life in physical ink!

💡 3 Golden Rules for Pen Plotting Beginners

  • No Fills, Only Strokes: A pen plotter cannot paint solid fills like a desktop printer. Shading is achieved by dense parallel lines, cross-hatching, or tight squiggles.
  • Mind the Pen Bleed: If a line stops for too long or overlaps repeatedly, wet ink can bleed through paper. Our TSP Path Optimizer eliminates duplicate visits!
  • Test Your Pen Height First: When clamping your pen (Sakura Micron, gel pen, or fountain pen), ensure the pen tip sits ~3mm above the paper when raised, and rests gently on paper when lowered.

🚀 Recommended Pens & Papers

  • Best Pens: Sakura Pigma Micron (0.3mm or 0.5mm), Uni-ball Signo Broad (White or Metallic Gold), Pilot G2 Gel.
  • Best Paper: Bristol Board (Smooth), Heavy Cardstock (250+ gsm), or Clairefontaine archival sketch paper.
